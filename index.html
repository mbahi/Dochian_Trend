How this is computed

Multi-timeframe strip — 1m/5m/15m/1H are streamed and analyzed independently, all the time, side by side. Each timeframe has its own genuinely different structure — switching the main chart doesn't "change" a setup, it's reading a different one. Use the strip to check for confluence across timeframes before you rely on any single one.
Swings — fractal highs/lows (2 bars either side). BOS — close breaks the last swing with the prevailing trend. CHoCH — close breaks against it, flipping the tracked bias.
FVG Confluence strategy — Entry at the near edge of an unmitigated 3-bar imbalance formed with the latest break, SL beyond the invalidating swing, TP1/TP2 at the next opposing swings.
Volatility Squeeze Breakout strategy — a Bollinger Band(20,2σ) compressed entirely inside a Keltner Channel(EMA20 ± 1.5×ATR20) marks a "squeeze" — volatility coiled tighter than normal. Once at least 6 consecutive bars have been squeezed and the bands then expand back outside the channel (the "release"), a break of the squeeze's own high or low in that direction is the entry. SL is ATR(20)×1.5; TP1/TP2 are a 1×/2× measured-move projection off the squeeze's own range height, not an arbitrary R:R — a tighter squeeze produces a smaller target, a wider one a bigger target. No H4 filter; self-contained on whichever timeframe tab is selected above. Only just added — treat every result as unverified until it accumulates real episodes.
Mean Reversion (Bollinger/RSI2) — the actual defined concept, per Connors' rigorously-backtested short-term reversal research: price must close beyond a 20-period Bollinger Band (2 standard deviations) AND the 2-period RSI must be at a genuine extreme (below 5 for a long, above 95 for a short) — both conditions, not either alone. A regime filter (H4 swing structure showing neither a clear HH/HL uptrend nor LH/LL downtrend) gates every signal, since Bollinger mean-reversion is documented to "walk the bands" and bleed out a string of losses during real trends — this filter exists specifically to avoid fading a strong trend. Target is the 20-period mid-band (the actual mean being reverted to) for TP1 (close 50%, SL→BE), with a modest stretch beyond it for TP2. Stop is ATR(14)×1.5. A 10-bar time-stop force-closes the trade if neither the stop nor target is hit by then, since mean-reversion setups that haven't resolved quickly usually aren't going to. Runs on whichever timeframe tab is selected above, unlike Trend Following's fixed H1/H4. Only just added — treat every result as unverified until it accumulates real episodes. Trend Following (Donchian) — the actual defined concept: H4 momentum sign (200-bar lookback) must agree with the breakout direction before anything is shown; entry is an H1 Donchian(20) channel breakout (price closing beyond the highest high/lowest low of the last 20 fully-closed bars, excluding the current one). Stop starts at ATR(14)×2, then trails at ATR(14)×3 and only ever tightens in the trade's favor, or the trade exits on a Donchian(10) breach against the position — whichever happens first. No fixed take-profit: TP1/TP2 shown are reference-only (1.5R/3R), not the real exit rule — this strategy's whole edge comes from letting winners run via the trail rather than capping them at a target, which is also why its backtest can't reuse the fixed-target R-multiple engine the other three strategies share; it tracks an actually open position bar-by-bar instead.
