How this is computed

Multi-timeframe strip — 1m/5m/15m/1H are streamed and analyzed independently, all the time, side by side. Each timeframe has its own genuinely different structure — switching the main chart doesn't "change" a setup, it's reading a different one. Use the strip to check for confluence across timeframes before you rely on any single one.
Swings — fractal highs/lows (2 bars either side). BOS — close breaks the last swing with the prevailing trend. CHoCH — close breaks against it, flipping the tracked bias.
StructureLedgerAI Sweep + FVG strategy — an M5 StructureLedgerAI liquidity sweep initiates every setup; nothing below matters until one occurs. Bullish sweep: an M5 candle's low breaks the prior candle's low, then closes back above it. Bearish sweep: the mirror on highs. Only the most recently CLOSED M5 candle counts — never the one still forming, so nothing repaints mid-candle. Once a sweep fires, a structure response (BOS/CHoCH) must close AFTER it, in the sweep's direction; then an unmitigated FVG must form AT/AFTER that structure break. Sequence, not a checklist — a fresh FVG with no preceding sweep does not qualify, and a sweep with no structure response afterward does not qualify either, however long it's been. Once that chain (sweep → structure → FVG) is intact, the FVG must also overlap a breaker block and agree with D1 trend before it's shown. Entry is the near edge of that FVG; SL beyond the invalidating swing; TP1/TP2 at the next opposing swings. The live status line reflects exactly how far the current sequence has progressed (watching for a sweep / sweep found, waiting on structure / structure confirmed, waiting on FVG / FVG found, checking confluence). Just rebuilt around this sequencing — treat every result as unverified until it accumulates real episodes.
Volatility Squeeze Breakout strategy — a Bollinger Band(20,2σ) compressed entirely inside a Keltner Channel(EMA20 ± 1.5×ATR20) marks a "squeeze" — volatility coiled tighter than normal. Once at least 6 consecutive bars have been squeezed and the bands then expand back outside the channel (the "release"), a break of the squeeze's own high or low in that direction is the entry. SL is ATR(20)×1.5; TP1/TP2 are a 1×/2× measured-move projection off the squeeze's own range height, not an arbitrary R:R — a tighter squeeze produces a smaller target, a wider one a bigger target. No H4 filter; self-contained on whichever timeframe tab is selected above. Only just added — treat every result as unverified until it accumulates real episodes.
Mean Reversion (Bollinger/RSI2) — the actual defined concept, per Connors' rigorously-backtested short-term reversal research: price must close beyond a 20-period Bollinger Band (2 standard deviations) AND the 2-period RSI must be at a genuine extreme (below 5 for a long, above 95 for a short) — both conditions, not either alone. A regime filter (H4 swing structure showing neither a clear HH/HL uptrend nor LH/LL downtrend) gates every signal, since Bollinger mean-reversion is documented to "walk the bands" and bleed out a string of losses during real trends — this filter exists specifically to avoid fading a strong trend. Target is the 20-period mid-band (the actual mean being reverted to) for TP1 (close 50%, SL→BE), with a modest stretch beyond it for TP2. Stop is ATR(14)×1.5. A 10-bar time-stop force-closes the trade if neither the stop nor target is hit by then, since mean-reversion setups that haven't resolved quickly usually aren't going to. Runs on whichever timeframe tab is selected above, unlike Trend Following's fixed H1/H4. Only just added — treat every result as unverified until it accumulates real episodes. Trend Following (Donchian) — the actual defined concept: H4 momentum sign (200-bar lookback) must agree with the breakout direction before anything is shown; entry is an H1 Donchian(20) channel breakout (price closing beyond the highest high/lowest low of the last 20 fully-closed bars, excluding the current one). Stop starts at ATR(14)×2, then trails at ATR(14)×3 and only ever tightens in the trade's favor, or the trade exits on a Donchian(10) breach against the position — whichever happens first. Optional (off by default) H4-confirmed fixed R:R target: once price touches the target intrabar, the stop tightens to breakeven immediately for protection, but the trade only actually closes for the full target on the H4 candle that CLOSES beyond it — same-bar adverse checks (stop/channel breach) are always resolved first, conservatively. With the target off, TP1/TP2 shown are reference-only (1.5R/3R), not the real exit rule — this strategy's whole edge otherwise comes from letting winners run via the trail rather than capping them at a target, which is also why its backtest can't reuse the fixed-target R-multiple engine the other three strategies share; it tracks an actually open position bar-by-bar instead.
